Solution overview / for review

Structured Note Smart Contract Issuance

The Agent acting as Issuing & Paying Agent, Registrar & Transfer Agent for a simulated native Structured Note issuance on Ethereum Sepolia testnet.

Status Draft for review Network Sepolia testnet Notional Zero, simulated Denomination 1 unit = €1,000 Cryptocurrency None

Requirement

Requirement

Issue a Structured Note via smart contract to initial investors, facilitate coupon payment, Issuer buy back, and trigger a redemption based on underlying reference asset value. The Note is serviced through to Mandatory Early Redemption or final redemption, with full reconciliation confirming zero positions.

Design basis

  • Identity Registry. On-chain investor whitelist, managed by the Agent via multi-signature, enforcing selling restrictions. Every component defers to it.
  • Cash leg. Settlement in a simulated tokenized deposit, so both legs of delivery versus payment are permissioned.
  • Register. Maintained off-chain by the Agent and authoritative. On-chain state is evidence of the Register and is reconciled against it.
Non-production proof of concept No cryptocurrency is involved in this exercise. Nothing here constitutes a security, a deposit, or an offer or solicitation. All instruments, participants and balances are simulated on a public test network and carry no notional value. Scope of simulation is set out in full below.

Rationale

Basis for tokenization

The question to answer is the upside for the Issuer and, separately, the upside for the investor. Existing infrastructure is well established and performs its function, so the case has to be made against it rather than in the abstract. Four areas account for most of the benefit, and each is measurable within this proof of concept.

DimensionCurrent arrangementThis design
Settlement Sequential delivery and payment through the ICSD, with a settlement cycle measured in days Atomic DvP. Both legs settle in a single transaction or neither does, removing the settlement window and the exposure within it
Operating hours Business days and cut-off times in the issuing centre Continuous. No cut-off and no dependency on the business day of any single centre, which matters most for short-dated notes where the timeline is already compressed
Inventory Tranching up, reducing down and buy back are three separate manual processes, with the Issuer holding a portfolio against them Derived from balances. Live and pending cannot diverge, reduction cannot reach notes outstanding in the market, and buy back returns notional without a separate instruction
Investor visibility Visibility stops at the ICSD. The Agent does not see the underlying holders The Register is the position. Holders of record are visible to the Agent continuously, and the coupon population is determined rather than requested
Selling restrictions Enforced at primary placement. Not systematically policed in the secondary market Enforced at every transfer, on-chain, before value moves. Reissuing under a different restriction is a change of required flags
Corporate actions Instruction, reconciliation and correction span multiple systems and counterparties, with touchpoints at each Determination, snapshot and distribution in one place, with reference asset data feeding the contract directly and triggers evaluated against it
Reconciliation Periodic and after the fact Continuous against on-chain balances, with zero positions provable at close

Why a shared public ledger

Several of the benefits above are obtainable on a private permissioned network, and institutions commonly begin there. Four are not, and they are the reasons to look at a shared ledger specifically rather than at distributed ledger technology generally.

  • Continuous operation across jurisdictions. These notes are distributed across EMEA, APAC and North America. A shared ledger operates without reference to the business day of the issuing centre, so settlement, transfer and distribution do not queue behind a time zone.
  • Liquidity from regulated payment tokens. Regulated euro-denominated payment tokens are issued on public networks. An onboarded investor holding one converts into the tokenized deposit without a wire instruction. That liquidity is not reachable from a closed network, and the conversion keeps the investor inside a permissioned perimeter instead of requiring settlement in an unregulated asset.
  • Composability with other authorised participants. A permissioned token can be used in transactions the Issuer did not design and is not required to support, by participants who are themselves whitelisted. One transaction is demonstrated here; the material value is in the scenarios other authorised participants construct against the same instrument. A network whose participant set and application set are both fixed at the outset cannot demonstrate this.
  • Neutrality of the venue. No participant operates the ledger, so no institution depends on the platform of a competitor to service its own issuance. This becomes material where issuing, paying and distributing parties compete with one another.

Portability is a further consideration rather than a benefit in itself. Institutions that have begun on a private EVM-compatible network retain that work, since the same contracts and the same tooling apply and moving to a public network is a change of target rather than a rebuild. Distribution is also extending: structured notes have historically been placed with high net worth investors through wealth divisions, and placement is broadening into digitally distributed channels. Instruments already settling on public infrastructure are positioned for that shift.

Open questions this exercise is intended to raise Existing infrastructure retains three advantages: an established legal framework for settlement finality, a central securities depository standing behind the position, and an investor base already onboarded to it. None of the three is a reason to defer the exercise. Each is a question that a working transaction states more precisely than a paper discussion can, and part of the purpose of the proof of concept is to put them in a form that can be worked.
Open questionWhere it stands todayWhat the exercise contributes
Settlement finality Conferred through an established legal framework operating via the depository A golden source hierarchy under which the Register remains authoritative, drafted for counsel, and a working mechanism against which an opinion can be given rather than a hypothetical one
Depository function A central securities depository stands behind the position. The Agent does not An explicit boundary on what the Agent does and does not assume, and a delegated registrar model demonstrating that authority can be extended to a further party without the Agent becoming a depository
Investor onboarding An investor base is already onboarded to existing infrastructure The onboarding model, whitelist governance and custodian treatment that a first live onboarding would use. The binding constraint here is distribution rather than technology

The merits of the underlying capability warrant working these through rather than leaving them to be resolved in the abstract. A transaction that has run end to end gives counsel, risk functions and distribution something specific to respond to, which is a materially better starting position than a design on paper.

Identity

Identity Registry

On-chain investor whitelist, managed by the Agent via multi-signature, enforcing selling restrictions. The Agent maintains the off-chain KYB and KYC register and operates the issuing and verifying services; the Identity Registry contract is the on-chain expression of what those services have determined. Every other component defers to this contract, which makes it the principal control in the design. Eligibility is established by presentation and proof of address control, and is granted for a period rather than indefinitely.

Credentials, and how eligibility is established

Eligibility is not asserted by the Agent on a participant's behalf. The participant presents it, proves control of the address it should attach to, and receives a grant that expires. Four credential types carry the exercise, each anchored on the entity rather than on any natural person, since the investors here are institutions.

CredentialSubjectCarries
Corporate identityThe investing entityLegal name, legal form, registration number, jurisdiction, incorporation date, status. Anchored on KYB
Corporate officerThe individual actingThe corporation it relates to, role, effective and termination dates, board resolution reference, scope of authority
Investor eligibilityThe investing entityRegulation S and Rule 144A eligibility, professional and accredited status, qualified institutional buyer status, jurisdiction, sanctions screening outcome. Determined by the Agent from the KYB file
Wallet controlThe addressChain, address, address type, attestation method and signature. Binds a specific address to the holder

Granular rights, and per-instrument gates

A grant is not a single permission. It records a set of rights as flags, covering hold, receive, transfer, swap and provide liquidity, alongside the attributes carrying regulatory meaning: Regulation S and Rule 144A eligibility, professional and accredited status, qualified institutional buyer status, jurisdiction, and sanctions screening outcome.

Each instrument and each venue then carries its own gate over the same registry, testing the rights that instrument requires. A Regulation S note requires the Regulation S flag, so a transfer to a counterparty without it reverts before value moves. Reissuing under Rule 144A changes the required mask rather than the contract. The conversion pool tests a different mask again, because swapping into a deposit is not the same permission as holding a security. Selling restrictions are not systematically enforced in the secondary market under current arrangements.

Time-boxed eligibility

A grant carries an expiry and lapses without any further action. This is a deliberate departure from a standing whitelist, and it answers a control weakness that a static list cannot: eligibility is re-established periodically rather than assumed indefinitely. A participant whose corporate status, sanctions position or investor classification has changed does not remain eligible because nobody revisited the entry. Renewal is a fresh presentation against the current credential.

Revocation is immediate and does not wait for expiry. The Agent withdraws a grant directly, and a credential revoked at the registry fails the next presentation. The combination means the on-chain answer can be wrong only within a bounded window, and the window is a parameter rather than an accident.

Credential platform, and why it is not yet fixed

The identity layer is described above by capability rather than by product. The design depends on what the layer does, not on who supplies it, and the selection remains open pending further discussion. Three routes are under consideration and the architecture accommodates each without rework to the instrument, the settlement path or the controls.

The requirements the layer must satisfy are the same in every case: entity and officer credentials rather than only natural-person identity, attributes the Agent determines from its own file, proof of address control including for contract accounts, delegated issuance scoped per registrar, revocation with bounded propagation, personal data held off-chain, and a check cheap enough to sit on a transfer path. The decision is deferred deliberately, and it is scoped as a workstream after the exercise, where distribution rather than technology is expected to be the deciding input.

Delegation to a custodian

A custody account is a contract and cannot hold a credential or answer a challenge. Two paths cover it, and both are supported by the attestation model rather than requiring an exception to it.

  • The account attests for itself. A custody account implementing the contract signature standard validates an attestation against its own logic, so the account proves control of itself under the custodian's operating key. The wallet control credential already distinguishes an externally owned address from a contract for this reason.
  • The custodian presents for a delegated client. The custodian's officer presents the client's eligibility credential together with evidence of the delegation, and the grant is written against the client's account. The Agent sees that the custodian acted and for which account, without receiving the client's underlying file.

In both cases the account, not the custodian, is the holder of record, which is what preserves the segregation the custody model exists to provide. Freezing or correcting one client account does not touch another under the same custodian.

Not written to the chain in any form at any point.

A party already holding the underlying record can verify that it matches the on-chain commitment. A party without the record derives nothing from the commitment. Jurisdiction is the one attribute held in the clear, as the contract requires it for the selling restriction test. The contract needs only to establish whether a participant falls within a permitted set rather than the specific jurisdiction, and a zero-knowledge set membership proof would replace the plain value in a production implementation.

Delegated authority

Registrar authority is scoped by construction. A registrar may bind and revoke only the participants it onboarded, and an attempt to act on another registrar's participants fails. The constraint is enforced twice by two independently authored systems, so the attempt fails in the registry contract and fails again in the credential layer beneath it.

This permits the Agent to delegate onboarding authority to a further institution without transferring its Register and without assuming central securities depository functions. A second registrar admits and revokes its own participants and co-signs intervention. The Agent observes that a participant was onboarded and by which registrar, and does not observe the participant's identity.

Where verification actually happens Cryptographic verification of a presented credential is performed off-chain, by the Agent's verifying service. The signature schemes used for verifiable credentials are prohibitively expensive to verify on-chain, and the composite on-chain check across issuer registration, revocation and root freshness is itself too costly to sit on a transfer path. Verification therefore runs at presentation, the result is written as a grant carrying rights and an expiry, and each subsequent transfer check is a single storage read. Claiming that a credential is cryptographically verified on-chain at every transfer would be inaccurate, and is the sort of claim a reviewer will test. The defensible claim is narrower and still strong: eligibility is established cryptographically, recorded on-chain, bounded in time, and revocable immediately.

Instrument

The payoff is the component not covered by any standard and not protected by any upstream audit, and is therefore specified in full before implementation. Specification includes the determinations that carry material consequence: whether the barrier test is strict or inclusive, whether a holder at the record date timestamp is included, and the direction of rounding.

Live and pending inventory

Notes held in an investor wallet are live. Notes returned to the Issuer are pending and are not cancelled. Both figures are derived from balances rather than maintained in a parallel ledger, so the two cannot diverge, and the programme cannot be reduced by notes outstanding in the market. Buy back returns notional to pending automatically without a separate instruction, consolidating two manual processes into a single contract call.

Lifecycle

Transaction lifecycle

  1. 01
    Governance Setup
    Agent appointed. Calculation & Determination Agent designated. Guarantee documented off-chain. Smart contracts deployed on Sepolia. Transaction details anchored in token metadata. Source code verified on Etherscan.
  2. 02
    KYB / KYC and whitelist
    The Agent completes KYB on the investing entity and KYC on its officers, then issues the corresponding credentials. The participant presents the claims required, proves control of the address, and receives a time-boxed grant carrying its rights. Sanctions screening is part of the check. No subsequent step can proceed for a party without a live grant.
  3. 03
    Issuance / Minting
    The Issuer instructs the Agent to authorise issuance. The Agent instructs the platform to create the contract. Strike Date Closing Levels are recorded on-chain as immutable Initial Levels. Notional mints to pending inventory, then settles to investors by DvP against digital monies.
  4. 04
    Secondary Transfer
    Identity Registry whitelist check, then atomic DvP against digital monies, then the Agent updates the on-chain and off-chain Register.
  5. 05
    Coupon Payment
    The Calculation Agent determines the coupon payable based on underlying reference assets. The contract is updated by determination, debits the Agent and credits whitelisted holders with digital monies. The Register is reconciled against on-chain balances.
  6. 06
    Issuer Buy Back
    The Issuer purchases the Note from an investor through a contract-triggered settlement. Notional returns to pending inventory.
  7. 07
    Autocall / MER Monitoring
    The Calculation Agent checks MER triggers. If triggered, the contract is updated by determination, debits the Agent and credits whitelisted holders with digital monies. Tokens are burned and the Register is closed.
  8. 08
    Redemption
    The Issuer determines the final scenario. Distribution is executed, tokens are burned, the Register is closed, and full reconciliation confirms zero positions.

Delivery versus payment

Placement, secondary transfer and buy back all settle through the same primitive. Both legs are tested against the Identity Registry before either moves, so an ineligible counterparty causes the whole settlement to fail rather than leaving one leg delivered against an unsettled other.

Record date determination

A secondary transfer settling between the record date and the payment date must not alter entitlement. This requires a balance at a past instant, which is why the token carries snapshot semantics rather than a current balance read. The same mechanism supports correction, since reversing a distribution requires a record of exactly which parties were paid.

Governance

Governance and intervention

Immutability is the principal question for a risk committee. Records cannot be altered without trace, which is the benefit, and records cannot be altered, which is the constraint. A single privileged key capable of arbitrary action is not an acceptable response and would be raised as an audit finding. Privileged capability is therefore separated by purpose.

Tier 2 is governed by two thresholds rather than one: a count of signatures and a count of distinct institutions. Four signatures from a single firm is a materially weaker control than two from each of two institutions, and a single threshold cannot express that distinction. The reason is stored as text rather than a hash, since a notice requiring an off-chain lookup does not constitute notice.

Scope of privileged functions The controller may call only a fixed, named set of functions. No general-purpose call path exists within it. An arbitrary-call function would constitute unrestricted capability irrespective of the surrounding process, and the fixed selector set is the distinction. The deploying key renounces its administrative rights as its final action, and that renunciation is verified under test.

Worked example: incorrect record date

A coupon is announced against an incorrect record date. The terms specify the 25th and the instruction states the 23rd. Holders of record on the 23rd are paid, so an investor who sold on the 24th has received a distribution on a position no longer held.

Correction proceeds as a Tier 2 intervention. A proposal is raised carrying the reason in writing. A second institution co-signs. A timelock runs, during which all participants can observe that an intervention is pending and on what grounds. Clawback is then executed, with each holder's reversal emitted individually against the original credit, and a second proposal restates the coupon to the correct record date. The original distribution remains on the ledger permanently, marked reversed, with the amount visible. History is restated rather than erased, and the amendment forms part of the record.

Cash leg

Digital monies

On-chain cash leg settlement is effected in a simulated tokenized deposit, modelled as a liability of the institution holding it, created on credit to an account and extinguished on debit. Mint authority sits behind a gateway interface standing in for a deposit service. The material output is that interface, which constitutes a specification of what such a service would be required to expose in order to settle on public infrastructure.

A permissioned conversion venue operates between a euro-denominated payment token and the deposit token, quoted in a narrow band around par. This provides a two-way price with a spread, consistent with a conversion facility, and does not represent independent price formation.

Euro payment token The conversion pair uses a euro-referenced payment token issued on the test network by a regulated electronic money issuer. It is fiat-referenced and redeemable at par, and is not a cryptocurrency, carrying no independent price exposure and being no part of the network's native asset. The testnet issuance has no monetary value and is distributed without charge by the issuer's faucet.
Perimeter of the conversion facility The whitelist check applies to the party swapping, prior to the pool. It does not apply to the asset. A wallet not onboarded cannot acquire the deposit token. This does not constitute open access to a bank deposit; it permits an onboarded investor to convert an external euro balance into a regulated deposit without a wire instruction. Characterising the facility as permissionless would be inaccurate.

Conversion venue: Uniswap v4 permissioned pool

The venue is a Uniswap v4 permissioned pool pairing the euro payment token against the tokenized deposit. A restricted token cannot be placed into the shared Pool Manager directly, because a claim against it could then be transferred outside the whitelist. The Permissions Adapter is instead the sole authorised holder of the deposit token and mints a virtual representation into the pool, while a hook attached at pool creation tests the swapping party against the Identity Registry on every swap.

Basis between the two credits

A pool between a third-party payment token and an institution's own deposit liability constitutes a basis position between two credits, which hold near par only while both redeem at par. With the institution as sole liquidity provider it stands ready to absorb one and issue the other. Mitigations are inventory caps on the adapter, a callable pause on swaps, and an amendable whitelist.

Standards

Open standards

Established open standards are used at every seam rather than bespoke formats, so that a custodian, an explorer, a venue or a counterparty's wallet can read the instruments and the credentials without integration work specific to this transaction. European digital identity

No European digital identity integration is built in this proof of concept, and none is simulated. This subsection records a design consideration: what the architecture already aligns with, what would have to be built, and why the timing makes it a near-term question rather than a distant one. It is included because the design was made not to foreclose it, and because a reader will ask.

eIDAS 2.0 requires every member state to offer an EU Digital Identity Wallet, with member state rollout due in November 2026. The regulation provides for Qualified Electronic Attestations of Attributes issued by regulated entities, banks among them. An investor eligibility credential of the kind used here is structurally exactly that.

Why this matters on this timeline The note is euro-denominated and distributed across EMEA. Within months, an EU investor may hold a member-state-issued attestation of corporate status or investor classification in a wallet the state provides. Accepting that as an onboarding input is a plausible near-term path, and the work to get there is a protocol bridge rather than a change to the credential model or the instrument. The position is that the bridges are specified and not implemented, that the European reference architecture is still iterating, and that building against a moving target would be premature for a proof of concept. Nothing in this area is claimed as delivered. The design does not foreclose it, and the work to reach it is scoped as a workstream after the exercise rather than inside it.

Programme

Programme structure

Eight phases. Second line approvals carry the longest lead time on the critical path and are initiated in phase one rather than when code is complete. The contract surface is frozen before assurance review so that the review is performed against a fixed version. The independent security audit is proposed as a gate before mainnet rather than before testnet execution, for the reasons set out under review and approval.

Approvals

Review, approval and documentation

The proof of concept carries no notional and creates no market exposure, so the review burden falls on conduct, data protection and technology risk rather than on market or credit risk. Second line functions are engaged at specification rather than at execution, since their findings change the design.

Proposed variation to the draft scope The draft specifies an independent smart contract security audit before proof of concept execution. We propose instead that the independent audit is a gate before mainnet, and that testnet execution is gated by an internal assurance review. The decision sits with the Agent and Technology Risk.

Assurance: two stages rather than one

The exercise carries zero notional and no client assets, so there is no value at risk for an audit to protect. More materially, the proof of concept exists to settle questions that will change the contracts: record date semantics, the nominee treatment of custodied holdings, the intervention surface, and the boundary conditions of the payoff. Auditing before those questions are answered means auditing a version that will not be the production version, and commissioning a second audit afterwards regardless. Audit lead times would also move execution by approximately a quarter without reducing any exposure that exists today.

StageGateRequirement
Before testnet Internal assurance review Security review against the specification, static analysis, the full negative scenario suite, a threat model covering key custody and the intervention surface, and declared dependency provenance including any release candidate
Before mainnet Independent security audit Full external audit against a frozen and tagged version informed by the proof of concept findings, with no unresolved high or critical findings, and re-review of any dependency promoted from release candidate to final

Recorded at mobilisation: the proof of concept artefacts are not promotable to production, and a production implementation re-enters the lifecycle at specification. Before testnet, the assurance burden is carried by the internal review and the negative scenario suite, which is the part of testing that demonstrates the controls bind.

The question Data Protection will ask first How a deletion request is satisfied against an immutable ledger. The answer in this design is that no personal data is written on-chain at any point, so the erasure obligation attaches only to the off-chain record, which is deletable in the ordinary way. The on-chain commitment becomes an unlinkable value once the salt and the record are destroyed. This should be documented rather than argued at the point of review.

Memorandum of understanding

A single MoU between the parties, executed at mobilisation, covering a non-binding exercise with no obligation on any party to proceed to production.

  • Parties and capacities, aligned to the responsibilities matrix above.
  • Scope and limitations. Simulated instrument, zero notional, test network only, no securities offered or sold, no client assets and no client data involved.
  • Intellectual property. Ownership of contracts, specification and documentation, and the licence granted to each party for internal use and for a production implementation.
  • Confidentiality and disclosure. What may be published, whether parties are named or anonymised, and approval rights over external communications. Existing non-disclosure arrangements referenced rather than restated.
  • Data protection. Whether any personal data is processed, controller and processor roles, and cross-border transfer. Drafted on the basis that no personal data leaves the institution, with a data processing agreement only if that changes.
  • Audit engagement. Which party contracts the audit firm, who bears the cost, and how findings are shared.
  • Costs. Each party bears its own, unless the audit is apportioned.
  • Term, termination and disposition of deployed artefacts and test data on completion.
  • Liability. Limited in proportion to a zero notional exercise, with no reliance placed on outputs for any live transaction.
  • Governing law and dispute resolution.

Conditions precedent to execution

None of the following constrain development. All constrain execution, and two carry lead times measured in weeks.

  • Internal assurance review completed with no unresolved high or critical findings, against a frozen and tagged contract version. The independent audit is a gate before mainnet, not before testnet execution.
  • Token naming sign-off. Name and symbol are permanently public and effectively immutable once deployed. The proposal is that no institutional or product name appears on-chain.
  • Golden source hierarchy recorded in the operating documentation, drafted for review and amendment by counsel. The mechanism is provided; the wording is not.
  • Settlement finality stated explicitly. Atomic settlement in a contract is not legal finality, which is conferred by the Register update. The two are not to be conflated in any external document.
  • Second line approvals obtained from Compliance, Financial Crime, Legal, Data Protection and Technology Risk.
  • Network upgrade scheduling. A gas repricing upgrade in early October affects tooling assuming fixed gas limits. Any rehearsal or execution window near that date is moved, or dry-run on an alternative test network.

After

After the proof of concept

The exercise is designed to produce a decision, not a platform. Immediate outputs are fixed; the workstreams that follow are conditional on that decision.

Immediate, within four weeks of execution

  • Findings report measured against the success criteria, with the evidence pack and the reconstructed lifecycle.
  • Decision pack setting out what was answered, what was scoped out, and what remains open for production.
  • Interface specification for the deposit service, derived from the gateway stub, describing what a production tokenized deposit would need to expose to settle on public infrastructure. This is the most reusable output of the exercise.
  • Operating documentation: runbook, incident procedures, and the golden source hierarchy as amended by counsel.
