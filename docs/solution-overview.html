Solution overview / for review

Structured Note Smart Contract Issuance

The Agent acting as Issuing & Paying Agent, Registrar & Transfer Agent for a simulated native Structured Note issued as an ERC-3643 token on Ethereum Sepolia, settled in the bank's tokenized deposit.

Status Draft for review Release 0.2.0 Network Ethereum Sepolia testnet Notional Zero, simulated Denomination 1 unit = €1,000 Cryptocurrency None

Requirement

Requirement

Issue a Structured Note via smart contract to initial investors, facilitate coupon payment, Issuer buy back, and trigger a redemption based on underlying reference asset value. The Note is serviced through to Mandatory Early Redemption or final redemption, with full reconciliation confirming zero positions.

Design basis

  • Identities and claims. Each client has one on-chain identity, and each institution vouches on it for what it is responsible for: the bank for its deposit, the onboarding agent for the note. Each instrument's identity registry decides, at every transfer, whether the claims it requires stand. This is ERC-3643, the standard of the bank's parallel proof of concept.
  • Cash leg. Settlement in the bank's tokenized deposit, an allowlist token whose eligibility comes from the same identities, so both legs of delivery versus payment are permissioned.
  • One template, a factory per deal. The supporting contracts are shared scaffolding, and a factory issues each note, whole, in one transaction from its term sheet.
  • Register. Maintained off-chain by the Agent and authoritative. On-chain state is evidence of the Register and is reconciled against it.
Non-production proof of concept No cryptocurrency is involved in this exercise. Nothing here constitutes a security, a deposit, or an offer or solicitation. All instruments, participants and balances are simulated on a public test network and carry no notional value. Scope of simulation is set out in full below.

Rationale

Basis for tokenization

The question to answer is the upside for the Issuer and, separately, the upside for the investor. Existing infrastructure is well established and performs its function, so the case has to be made against it rather than in the abstract. Four areas account for most of the benefit, and each is measurable within this proof of concept.

DimensionCurrent arrangementThis design
Settlement Sequential delivery and payment through the ICSD, with a settlement cycle measured in days Atomic DvP. Both legs settle in a single transaction or neither does, removing the settlement window and the exposure within it
Operating hours Business days and cut-off times in the issuing centre Continuous. No cut-off and no dependency on the business day of any single centre, which matters most for short-dated notes where the timeline is already compressed
Inventory Tranching up, reducing down and buy back are three separate manual processes, with the Issuer holding a portfolio against them Derived from balances. Live and pending cannot diverge, reduction cannot reach notes outstanding in the market, and buy back returns notional without a separate instruction
Investor visibility Visibility stops at the ICSD. The Agent does not see the underlying holders The Register is the position. Holders of record are visible to the Agent continuously, and the coupon population is determined rather than requested
Selling restrictions Enforced at primary placement. Not systematically policed in the secondary market Enforced at every transfer, on-chain, before value moves. Reissuing under a different restriction is a change of the claim the note requires
Corporate actions Instruction, reconciliation and correction span multiple systems and counterparties, with touchpoints at each Determination, record dates and distribution in one place, with reference asset data feeding the contract directly and triggers evaluated against it
Reconciliation Periodic and after the fact Continuous against on-chain balances, with zero positions provable at close

Why a shared public ledger

Several of the benefits above are obtainable on a private permissioned network, and institutions commonly begin there. Four are not, and they are the reasons to look at a shared ledger specifically rather than at distributed ledger technology generally.

  • Continuous operation across jurisdictions. These notes are distributed across EMEA, APAC and North America. A shared ledger operates without reference to the business day of the issuing centre, so settlement, transfer and distribution do not queue behind a time zone.
  • Liquidity from regulated payment tokens. Regulated euro-denominated payment tokens are issued on public networks. An onboarded investor holding one converts into the tokenized deposit without a wire instruction. That liquidity is not reachable from a closed network, and the conversion keeps the investor inside a permissioned perimeter instead of requiring settlement in an unregulated asset.
  • Composability with other authorised participants. A permissioned token can be used in transactions the Issuer did not design and is not required to support, by participants who are themselves whitelisted. One transaction is demonstrated here; the material value is in the scenarios other authorised participants construct against the same instrument. A network whose participant set and application set are both fixed at the outset cannot demonstrate this.
  • Neutrality of the venue. No participant operates the ledger, so no institution depends on the platform of a competitor to service its own issuance. This becomes material where issuing, paying and distributing parties compete with one another.

Portability is a further consideration rather than a benefit in itself. Institutions that have begun on a private EVM-compatible network retain that work, since the same contracts and the same tooling apply and moving to a public network is a change of target rather than a rebuild. Distribution is also extending: structured notes have historically been placed with high net worth investors through wealth divisions, and placement is broadening into digitally distributed channels. Instruments already settling on public infrastructure are positioned for that shift.

Open questions this exercise is intended to raise Existing infrastructure retains three advantages: an established legal framework for settlement finality, a central securities depository standing behind the position, and an investor base already onboarded to it. None of the three is a reason to defer the exercise. Each is a question that a working transaction states more precisely than a paper discussion can, and part of the purpose of the proof of concept is to put them in a form that can be worked.
Open questionWhere it stands todayWhat the exercise contributes
Settlement finality Conferred through an established legal framework operating via the depository A golden source hierarchy under which the Register remains authoritative, drafted for counsel, and a working mechanism against which an opinion can be given rather than a hypothetical one
Depository function A central securities depository stands behind the position. The Agent does not An explicit boundary on what the Agent does and does not assume, and a delegated registrar model demonstrating that authority can be extended to a further party without the Agent becoming a depository
Investor onboarding An investor base is already onboarded to existing infrastructure The onboarding model, claim governance and custodian treatment that a first live onboarding would use. The binding constraint here is distribution rather than technology

The merits of the underlying capability warrant working these through rather than leaving them to be resolved in the abstract. A transaction that has run end to end gives counsel, risk functions and distribution something specific to respond to, which is a materially better starting position than a design on paper.

Architecture

System architecture

An instruction originates in institution systems, crosses an integration layer, and settles on the shared ledger. The integration layer is the site's five portals, each signing as the institution whose role it is; it is built for this transaction, and it is where the boundary on personal data is enforced.

Identity

Identities and claims

Each client has one on-chain identity, and each institution vouches on it, by a signed claim, for what it is responsible for: the bank that the client may hold its deposit, the onboarding agent that the client may hold the note. Each asset's identity registry then decides, at every transfer, whether the claims it requires stand. This is ERC-3643, with ONCHAINID identities, the standard of the bank's parallel proof of concept. The institutions keep their KYB and KYC files; the chain carries only the claim that each has vouched, until when, and a salted hash of the record behind it. Eligibility is vouched for by the institution responsible, checked on-chain at every transfer, and granted for a period rather than indefinitely.

Claims, and how eligibility is established

A claim is an institution's signed statement about an identity: a topic, the issuer that vouches, the claim's issue and its lapse, a salted hash of the institution's own record, and the institution's signature over all of it. The client consents first, by giving the institution a claim key on its identity, and the institution then adds its claim.

Claims by role, and a registry per asset

Each asset names the claims it requires and the issuers it trusts for them. The note requires the onboarding agent's note claim, so a transfer to an account without it reverts before value moves. Reissuing under another selling restriction means requiring another claim, or trusting another issuer, which is a Tier 2 act on the note's registry rather than a change of contract. The deposit requires the bank's claim alone: the bank alone decides who may hold its cash. The pool requires the bank's pool-access claim, because converting into a deposit is not the same permission as holding a security. Selling restrictions are not systematically enforced in the secondary market under current arrangements.

Time-boxed eligibility

A claim carries its lapse and stops counting without any further action. This is a deliberate departure from a standing whitelist, and it answers a control weakness that a static list cannot: eligibility is re-established periodically rather than assumed indefinitely. A participant whose corporate status, sanctions position or investor classification has changed does not remain eligible because nobody revisited the entry. Renewal is a fresh claim, against the institution's current record.

Revocation is immediate and does not wait for the lapse: the institution that gave a claim revokes it, and the next transfer that relies on it is refused. No refresh, no cached answer, and no window between revocation and effect.

Not written to the chain in any form at any point.

A party already holding the record and its salt can check it against the hash a claim carries. A party without them derives nothing from the hash, and a fresh salt at each screening makes two screenings of one client unlinkable. Country is the one attribute held in the clear, as the pool requires it for the jurisdictions it serves. A production implementation could replace the plain value with a proof of membership of a permitted set.

Delegated authority

Authority is scoped by construction. A claim can be revoked only by the issuer that made it, and each asset names the issuers it trusts and for what. The onboarding agent vouches for its own clients for the note; it cannot vouch for the deposit, which trusts the bank alone, and the bank cannot vouch for the note.

This permits the Agent to rely on further institutions without transferring its Register and without assuming central securities depository functions. The Agent sees each participant as a wallet, an identity and the issuers that vouched for it, and does not see the participant's facts, which stay in the bank's records.

Where verification actually happens The judgement of a client, its KYB and its screening, is made off-chain, by the institution responsible, against its own file. What the chain checks, at every transfer, is the institution's claim: that it is present, signed by a key of an issuer the asset trusts, not lapsed and not revoked. That check is an ordinary signature recovery and a few storage reads, cheap enough to sit on a transfer path. The defensible claim is precise: eligibility is vouched for by the institution responsible, checked on-chain at every transfer, bounded in time, and revocable immediately.

Instrument

Instrument terms

The payoff is the component not covered by any standard and not protected by any upstream audit, and is therefore specified in full before implementation. Specification includes the determinations that carry material consequence: whether the barrier test is strict or inclusive, whether a holder at the record date timestamp is included, and the direction of rounding.

The term sheet as data

The term sheet is a typed record, not a document beside the note. Every field carries a value or an explicit "not applicable", with its reason, and a field that may not be "not applicable" must carry a value. The fields are a catalogue, each with an identifier, a type, a priority and an example, ready to take the bank's own field list by editing the catalogue alone.

The record is the input to issuance: the terms the Determination Agent fixes on the chain are derived from it, and nowhere else. Its hash, keccak256 of its canonical JSON (RFC 8785), is carried by the note's own identity with the address the site publishes it at, behind sign-in. The reconciliation statement checks, at any block, that the record and the terms on the chain agree field by field.

Live and pending inventory

Notes held in an investor wallet are live. Notes returned to the Issuer are pending and are not cancelled. Both figures are derived from balances rather than maintained in a parallel ledger, so the two cannot diverge, and the programme cannot be reduced by notes outstanding in the market. Buy back returns notional to pending automatically without a separate instruction, consolidating two manual processes into a single contract call.

Lifecycle

Transaction lifecycle

  1. 01
    Governance setup
    Agent appointed. Calculation & Determination Agent designated. Guarantee documented off-chain. The shared scaffolding deployed on Ethereum Sepolia: identities, the ERC-3643 implementations behind a fixed authority, the claim issuers, the deposit and the note factory. The institutions' multi-signature wallets created and the governance controller deployed. Source code verified on the block explorer.
  2. 02
    KYB / KYC and claims
    The bank confirms the client's KYB from its own record, creates its identity and vouches for its deposit. The client asks for the note; the onboarding agent verifies it under the selling restrictions and vouches for it. No subsequent step can proceed for a party without the claims the asset requires.
  3. 03
    Issuance / Minting
    The Issuer issues the note through the note factory, in one transaction, from its term sheet: terms and calendar, the token and its registries, the Paying Agent and settlement, every agent restricted, the notional minted to the Issuer's inventory, and the note opened. The bank allowlists the lifecycle contracts and funds the Agent. Units are placed with investors by delivery versus payment against the deposit.
  4. 04
    Secondary transfer
    By locked offer: both parties' eligibility checked, then the note and the deposit settle together, each side locked until it does. The on-chain register is the position; the Agent's Register is reconciled against it.
  5. 05
    Coupon payment
    The Calculation Agent determines the date from the underlying levels, signed and recomputed on-chain. At the Payment Date the Agent's servicing wallet draws the whole amount from the Agent's deposit and pays the holders of record at the Record Time.
  6. 06
    Issuer buy back
    The Issuer buys units back by locked offer. Notional returns to its inventory, which is not paid coupons and is never cancelled beyond what it holds.
  7. 07
    Autocall / MER
    A date that could redeem the note stops transfers from its Record Time until it is determined. If it redeems, the note terminates and the dates after it fall away; the redemption pauses the note, burns each holder's units as it pays, and burns the inventory at the close.
  8. 08
    Redemption and closure
    The final date determines the scenario. Distribution is executed, tokens are burned, and the reconciliation statement reads Closed: every position is at zero.

The order of events

The order is the contracts' own, and a test walks one note through it in that order, trying each step too early or out of turn first and showing the refusal. The full sequence, step by step with who acts and what is refused, is kept with the code (docs/lifecycle-sequence.md), ready to be fitted to the bank's own order of events. The order within a date is no more rigid than the contracts make it: a date may be determined before its Record Time, and transfers then never stop for it unless it redeems.

Delivery versus payment

Placement, secondary transfer and buy back all settle through the same primitive, an offer locked until it settles or ends. The seller's units are frozen in its own wallet, never moved into the contract; both legs are tested before either moves, so an ineligible counterparty causes the whole settlement to fail rather than leaving one leg delivered against an unsettled other.

Record dates

A secondary transfer settling between the Record Time and the Payment Date must not alter entitlement. The note's compliance therefore carries a record-date module, which records every balance at every past instant; the Paying Agent pays each holder its balance at the Record Time, never a figure supplied with the call. The same record supports correction, since reversing a distribution requires a record of exactly which parties were paid.

Governance

Governance and intervention

Immutability is the principal question for a risk committee. Records cannot be altered without trace, which is the benefit, and records cannot be altered, which is the constraint. A single privileged key capable of arbitrary action is not an acceptable response and would be raised as an audit finding. Every privileged power over the note is therefore held by one governance controller, separated by purpose into two tiers, and each agent of the note is limited by the token itself to the powers it needs.

Tier 1 / Operational

Register correction

The Register is authoritative and on-chain state is evidence of it. Where the two diverge, through a lost key or a mis-directed transfer, on-chain state is corrected to match the Register: a correction by forced transfer, a lost wallet's units and identity recovered to a new wallet, and the supervisory freeze, whichever institution vouched for the account.

Tier 2 / Intervention

Break glass

Amendment to the Register itself or to the economics of the instrument: reversal and restatement of a distribution, amendment of notional, whose claims the note trusts, and replacement of the servicing wallet or an institution's wallet. There is no upgrade: the note's implementation is fixed by an authority no one owns.

Authority
Quorum across two institutions
Delay
Timelock, with on-chain notice, then a window to execute
Requires
Written reason, emitted in full

Tier 2 is governed by two thresholds rather than one: a count of signatures and a count of distinct institutions. Four signatures from a single firm is a materially weaker control than two from each of two institutions, and a single threshold cannot express that distinction. The reason is stored as text rather than a hash, since a notice requiring an off-chain lookup does not constitute notice. A queued proposal left unexecuted past its window expires.

Scope of privileged functions The controller may perform only a fixed, named set of typed acts, and builds every call itself from their arguments. No general-purpose call path exists within it. The note factory owns a note for the Issuer from issuance, and hands it to the controller in one call at the ceremony; the deploying key renounces its rights as its final action, and that renunciation is verified under test.

Worked example: incorrect record date

A coupon is announced against an incorrect record date. The terms specify the 25th and the instruction states the 23rd. Holders of record on the 23rd are paid, so an investor who sold on the 24th has received a distribution on a position no longer held.

Correction proceeds as a Tier 2 intervention. A proposal is raised carrying the reason in writing. A second institution co-signs. A timelock runs, during which all participants can observe that an intervention is pending and on what grounds. Clawback is then executed, with each holder's reversal emitted individually against the original credit, and a second proposal restates the coupon to the correct record date. The original distribution remains on the ledger permanently, marked reversed, with the amount visible. History is restated rather than erased, and the amendment forms part of the record.

Cash leg

Digital monies

The cash leg is the bank's tokenized deposit, simulated: a liability of the bank, created on credit to a client's account and extinguished on debit, each against a booking reference the bank's core systems would carry. It is shaped like the one bank deposit token whose source is public: an allowlist ERC-20 with two decimals, checking sender, receiver and spender alike. Its eligibility comes from the bank's own identity registry, so a client onboarded once may hold both the deposit and the note, while the bank alone decides who may hold its cash. Contracts such as the Paying Agent and the settlement contract are on the explicit allowlist.

PauseEvery transfer, credit and debit stops at once, and with them the pool and settlement. A distribution meanwhile is held for each holder, to be claimed
Move by orderA court's or a regulator's order, the bank's alone, its written reason emitted in full

The gateway stands in for a deposit service, and is a material output in itself: a specification of what such a service would be required to expose in order to settle on public infrastructure.

A permissioned conversion venue, deployed by default, operates between a euro-denominated payment token and the deposit, quoted in a narrow band around par. This provides a two-way price with a spread, consistent with a conversion facility, and does not represent independent price formation.

Euro payment token The conversion pair uses a euro-referenced payment token issued on the test network by a regulated electronic money issuer. It is fiat-referenced and redeemable at par, and is not a cryptocurrency, carrying no independent price exposure and being no part of the network's native asset. The testnet issuance has no monetary value and is distributed without charge by the issuer's faucet.
Perimeter of the conversion facility The check applies to the party swapping, prior to the pool. It does not apply to the asset. A wallet converts only if the deposit accepts it and the bank has admitted it to the pool with its pool-access claim, its country is one the pool serves, and supervision has not withdrawn it. This does not constitute open access to a bank deposit; it permits an onboarded investor to convert an external euro balance into a regulated deposit without a wire instruction. Characterising the facility as permissionless would be inaccurate.

Conversion venue: Uniswap v4 permissioned pool

The venue is a Uniswap v4 permissioned pool pairing the euro payment token against the tokenized deposit. A restricted token cannot be placed into the shared Pool Manager directly, because a claim against it could then be transferred outside the perimeter. The Permissions Adapter is instead the sole authorised holder of the deposit and mints a virtual representation into the pool, while a hook attached at pool creation tests the swapping party on every swap: against the deposit's own eligibility, and against the pool's gate, which reads the pool's own ERC-3643 identity registry.

Basis between the two credits

A pool between a third-party payment token and an institution's own deposit liability constitutes a basis position between two credits, which hold near par only while both redeem at par. With the bank as sole liquidity provider it stands ready to absorb one and issue the other. Mitigations are the bank's own inventory in the pool, a switch on swaps held by the Agent's servicing wallet, the supervisory withdrawal of any participant, and the bank's revocation of its pool-access claims.

European digital identity

No European digital identity integration is built in this proof of concept, and none is simulated. This subsection records a design consideration: what the architecture already allows, what would have to be built, and why the timing makes it a near-term question.

eIDAS 2.0 requires every member state to offer an EU Digital Identity Wallet, with member state rollout due in November 2026, and provides for Qualified Electronic Attestations of Attributes issued by regulated entities, banks among them. An attestation of corporate status or investor classification is the kind of input an institution relies on before it vouches.

Why this matters on this timeline The note is euro-denominated and distributed across EMEA. Within months, an EU investor may hold a member-state-issued attestation in a wallet the state provides. In this design that attestation is an input to an institution's judgement, off-chain, before it signs its claim, so accepting it is an integration at the institution's portal rather than a change to the identities, the claims or the instrument. Nothing in this area is claimed as delivered. The design does not foreclose it, and the work is scoped as a workstream after the exercise.

Approvals

Review, approval and documentation

The proof of concept carries no notional and creates no market exposure, so the review burden falls on conduct, data protection and technology risk rather than on market or credit risk. Second line functions are engaged at specification rather than at execution, since their findings change the design.

Proposed variation to the draft scope The draft specifies an independent smart contract security audit before proof of concept execution. We propose instead that the independent audit is a gate before mainnet, and that testnet execution is gated by an internal assurance review. The decision sits with the Agent and Technology Risk.

Assurance: two stages rather than one

The exercise carries zero notional and no client assets, so there is no value at risk for an audit to protect. More materially, the proof of concept exists to settle questions that will change the contracts: record date semantics, the nominee treatment of custodied holdings, the intervention surface, and the boundary conditions of the payoff. Auditing before those questions are answered means auditing a version that will not be the production version, and commissioning a second audit afterwards regardless. Audit lead times would also move execution by approximately a quarter without reducing any exposure that exists today.

The question Data Protection will ask first How a deletion request is satisfied against an immutable ledger. The answer in this design is that no personal data is written on-chain at any point, so the erasure obligation attaches only to the off-chain record, which is deletable in the ordinary way. The hash a claim carries becomes an unlinkable value once the salt and the record are destroyed. This should be documented rather than argued at the point of review.

Memorandum of understanding

A single MoU between the parties, executed at mobilisation, covering a non-binding exercise with no obligation on any party to proceed to production.

  • Parties and capacities, aligned to the responsibilities matrix above.
  • Scope and limitations. Simulated instrument, zero notional, test network only, no securities offered or sold, no client assets and no client data involved.
  • Intellectual property. Ownership of contracts, specification and documentation, and the licence granted to each party for internal use and for a production implementation.
  • Confidentiality and disclosure. What may be published, whether parties are named or anonymised, and approval rights over external communications. Existing non-disclosure arrangements referenced rather than restated.
  • Data protection. Whether any personal data is processed, controller and processor roles, and cross-border transfer. Drafted on the basis that no personal data leaves the institution, with a data processing agreement only if that changes.
  • Audit engagement. Which party contracts the audit firm, who bears the cost, and how findings are shared.
  • Costs. Each party bears its own, unless the audit is apportioned.
  • Term, termination and disposition of deployed artefacts and test data on completion.
  • Liability. Limited in proportion to a zero notional exercise, with no reliance placed on outputs for any live transaction.
  • Governing law and dispute resolution.

Conditions precedent to execution

None of the following constrain development. All constrain execution, and two carry lead times measured in weeks.

  • Internal assurance review completed with no unresolved high or critical findings, against a frozen and tagged contract version. The independent audit is a gate before mainnet, not before testnet execution.
  • Token naming sign-off. Name and symbol are permanently public and effectively immutable once deployed. The proposal is that no institutional or product name appears on-chain.
  • Golden source hierarchy recorded in the operating documentation, drafted for review and amendment by counsel. The mechanism is provided; the wording is not.
  • Settlement finality stated explicitly. Atomic settlement in a contract is not legal finality, which is conferred by the Register update. The two are not to be conflated in any external document.
  • Second line approvals obtained from Compliance, Financial Crime, Legal, Data Protection and Technology Risk.
