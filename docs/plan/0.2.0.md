# Plan for 0.2.0

Written 14 October 2026, after the working group's call of 13 October. 0.2.0 is what the bank's IT
team receives: the code on GitHub, and a PDF of the solution overview.

## What the call settled

- **The bank's side runs on a standalone virtual machine**, not through the bank's core
  connectivity, which would need a long approval chain. The bank's IT team builds and runs it,
  partly through an AI-led development process, from our requirements, our PDF and our code.
- **The structured note moves from ERC-7943 to ERC-3643**, to align with a parallel proof of
  concept at the bank.
- **One master template and a factory.** The supporting contracts (identity, onboarding,
  administration) are shared scaffolding, and a factory issues a token for each deal. End users
  only see the token in their wallet.
- **The settlement asset is a tokenized deposit, not a stablecoin.** No stablecoin and no
  cryptocurrency touch the transaction. A tokenized deposit is an allowlist token, where a
  stablecoin uses a denylist.
- **The success criteria are the bank's nine:**
  1. full deal lifecycle execution;
  2. the issuance cycle;
  3. a coupon;
  4. a secondary transfer;
  5. KYC whitelist enforcement;
  6. registry reconciliation;
  7. day-to-day reconciliation;
  8. issuer buy-back mechanics;
  9. logic validation.
- **The bank is sending a list of the term-sheet fields** it must capture. Each comes with an
  identifier, examples, a data type and a priority (must, should or could have). Any field must be
  able to say "not applicable".
- **The bank is documenting the order of lifecycle events** in more detail.

## Decisions taken for 0.2.0 (owner, 14 October 2026)

Each becomes an ADR when it is built.

1. **ERC-3643 through T-REX,** the reference implementation from the ERC-3643 Association.
   - It is audited, and is most likely what the parallel proof of concept uses.
   - Our lifecycle contracts are adapted around it.
   - T-REX is GPL-3.0 and this repository MPL-2.0. The difference is stated plainly for the bank's legal review.
2. **One identity per client, with claims by role.**
   - **The identity.** Each client has one ONCHAINID, a small proxy contract created once, reused across the deposit and the note, and across all of the client's wallets.
   - **What each token accepts.** Each token names the claims it requires (Claim Topics) and whose claims it accepts (Trusted Issuers):
     - The **bank** vouches for its deposit clients: KYB, sanctions.
     - An **onboarding agent** (a dealer or distributor, a CSD, or the investor's custodian, played by a mock party) vouches for the note: Reg S eligibility, investor class. With the client's consent, it accepts the bank's existing KYB claim instead of repeating it.
   - **What the Agent sees.** As Issuing & Paying Agent and registrar, the Agent sees wallets, identities and which issuers vouched for each, never personal data. Claims carry only a hash of the verified record, kept by the party that checked it.
   - **Who holds an identity's key** follows custody:
     - the bank, for its custody platform's clients;
     - a third-party custodian, for its clients;
     - the client, for self-custody.

     Whoever holds the key authorises the parties that add claims.
   - **Portals.** The site separates five: the bank, the onboarding agent, the Agent, custodians, and the client. The client's journey becomes "onboarded for the deposit, then request access to the note".
   - **Licensing** (whether the onboarding agent must be a licensed CSD) is left open for the bank's markets and legal teams.
3. **The EURC conversion pool becomes an optional appendix,** deployable but off by default.
   Settlement is the tokenized deposit alone. S-27 to S-29 move to the appendix.
   *Amended by the owner, 14 October 2026 (ADR 0090): the pool is deployed by default, gated on the same ONCHAINID identities. Settlement is still the tokenized deposit alone, and S-27 to S-29 are back in the main scenario.*
4. **Everything moves to Ethereum Sepolia.**
   - The bank's virtual machine runs the site and calls out to public Ethereum Sepolia; the note and the deposit are permissioned tokens on a public network. A private chain would show none of the composability that is the reason for a shared ledger.
   - **Our public demo keeps working standalone,** on the same code and the same standards.
     - **Two deployments.** Ours and the bank's are each a complete stack, with their own keys and records, on the same network. The bank's never depends on ours, nor ours on the bank's.
     - **One codebase.** The same code builds the public site and the bank's container image; only configuration differs.
   - Base Sepolia is retired, its manifest kept.
   - Our tests stay on anvil.

5. **The external credential platform is removed entirely (owner, 14 October 2026).**
   - **What goes.** No external credential service, DID method, schema or anchoring registry remains in the demo, the virtual machine bundle or the documents.
   - **What replaces it.** Onboarding rests on ERC-3643's own identities and claims (decision 2).
   - **How far it reaches.** The review below found the platform in onboarding, in the pool's gate and in the deploy scripts, but not in the note's contracts.

## Removing the credential platform: what it touches

| Where | What it does with the platform today | What replaces it |
|---|---|---|
| Adding a client (`clients.ts`, `client-claims.ts`, `bank.ts`) | The bank's issuer server signs and anchors a corporate identity from the facts staff enter | The bank's portal keeps the facts off-chain, and its claim issuer adds a KYB claim, carrying the record's hash, to the client's identity |
| Wallet control (`bank.ts`, `service.ts`, `custody.ts`) | The platform mints a wallet-control credential once the wallet signs its challenge | The portal's own typed challenge, checked by signature (EIP-1271 for custody accounts); the Identity Registry then links the wallet to the identity (Q4) |
| The holder-wallet route (`protocol.ts`, `canonical.ts`, `assess.ts`) | An officer presents credentials by QR to the platform's verifier, with post-quantum signatures | An existing identity, brought to the note (Q2) |
| Anchoring (`anchoring.ts`) | Grants wait about a minute for the platform's batch root | Nothing to wait for: a claim is valid once its issuer signs it, and is revoked by that issuer at once |
| Assessment and grants (`assess.ts`, `grants.ts`) | The Access Issuer turns presentations into a pool grant and a registry grant | The onboarding parties add claims, and the token's Identity Registry and compliance decide |
| The pool's gate (`PoolCredentialRegistry.sol`) | Reads the platform's issuer and revocation registries | The pool reads the same identities (Q5; done, ADR 0090) |
| Deploying (`00_DeployBase.s.sol`, `setup-credential-protocol.sh`, `Attest.s.sol`) | Installs the platform's registries locally, and registers an issuer | T-REX's identity factory and claim issuers |
| Settings and secrets (`vercel-admission-env.sh`, key files) | The issuer server's address and key, the verifier, DIDs, schemas | Only the chain, the keys and the mail |
| Names | Its DID method name in 7 files; 15 ADRs rest on the platform | Removed from the code and current documents (Q6) |

What follows, with no decision needed:
- **Faster onboarding.** The anchoring wait goes, so a wallet is eligible as soon as its claims are added.
- **S-05 is fully built.** Under ERC-3643 a claim can be revoked only by the issuer that made it, and each token names its trusted issuers, so the credential layer's scoping is native.
- **No post-quantum signatures.** They leave with the platform: claims are ordinary Ethereum signatures. The overview's simulation table says otherwise and is corrected.
- **The virtual machine needs no outside service** but the Ethereum Sepolia RPC and, for sign-in, mail. Mail inside the bank may not leave, so the bundle can print sign-in codes to its log instead (codes replace links, ADR 0093).

## Questions for the owner

**Answered 14 October 2026: the recommendation on every question.** Q6 is fixed forward. The
questions are kept below as the record of what was weighed.

**Q1. What carries a client's verified facts, now that verifiable credentials go?**
- *Recommended:* **ERC-3643 claims, natively.**
  - Each onboarding party (the bank, the onboarding agent) has a claim issuer contract.
  - The verified record stays off-chain in the portal of the party that checked it.
  - The claim on the client's identity carries only the record's hash and the issuer's signature.
- *Alternative:* keep W3C verifiable credentials off-chain through an open-source library, beside the claims. That is a second system saying the same thing, and a dependency in the bank's virtual machine.

**Q2. What becomes of the holder-wallet route,** where an officer presents credentials by QR?
- *Recommended:* **"bring an existing identity".**
  - A client whose identity already carries claims from a trusted issuer (another bank, or the deposit bank) asks for access.
  - It proves control of its wallet with one signature.
  - The onboarding agent relies on those claims rather than checking again.
  - This is the reuse across issuers that ERC-3643 is built for, and the composability argument for a public network.
- *Alternative:* drop the route, leaving bank custody, third-party custody and self-custody, each onboarded directly.

**Q3. How is eligibility time-boxed?**
- *Background.* Today every grant expires (the 30-day screening, and a maximum validity), and the overview calls that a deliberate control. ERC-3643 claims do not expire by themselves.
- *Recommended:* **our claim issuer contract, extending T-REX's.**
  - Its validity check also refuses a claim past the expiry written into it, so eligibility lapses with no transaction, as today.
  - Revocation stays immediate.
- *Alternative:* standard T-REX behaviour, with no expiry. Eligibility then lasts until revoked, and re-screening is a process outside the chain. The overview's time-boxing control would be dropped.

**Q4. How does a client prove it controls a wallet?**
- *Recommended:* **a typed signature (EIP-712)** on a challenge the onboarding party's portal issues, checked by signature recovery, or EIP-1271 for a custody account. The Identity Registry then links the wallet to the client's identity. One signature, no fee for the client, as today.
- *Alternative:* the wallet adds itself as a key on its identity by its own transaction. That is more on-chain, but needs ETH in the client's wallet and a transaction in MetaMask.

**Q5. What gates the appendix pool,** whose own gate reads the platform's registries?
- *Recommended:* **the pool reads the same ERC-3643 identities** (a pool-access claim from the bank) through a small gate that replaces `PoolCredentialRegistry`. The platform-coupled gate is deleted.
- *Alternative:* leave the appendix as it is. That keeps the platform in the codebase, contradicting its removal.

**Q6. What about the platform's name and its ADRs?**
- *Recommended:* **fix forward.**
  - Remove its name and DID method from the code and the current documents.
  - Keep the ADRs that rested on it as history: marked superseded by the new ADRs, and naming the platform only generically.
  - Leave the git history as it is (the platform's product name itself is already out of it).
- *Alternative:* also rewrite the history to remove the DID method name. Every commit would be re-signed, and v0.1.0 tagged again.

## Workstreams, in order

0. **Research, then review with the owner before building.**
   - **The tokenized deposit's design:**
     - JPMD's verified source on Base;
     - other global banks' deposit tokens on public networks;
     - OpenZeppelin's current practice (allowlists, AccessManager, pausing, the community ERC-7943 and allowlist extensions).

     Then a recommendation for the deposit token: whether it is ERC-3643 itself, or an allowlist ERC-20 that reads the same identities.
   - **What we depend on, on Ethereum Sepolia:**
     - Safe and Permit2 at their canonical addresses;
     - EURC and Uniswap v4, for the appendix.
   - **T-REX:** the version, its agent model and its licence.

   **Done (14 October 2026):**
   - **Ethereum Sepolia.** Safe 1.4.1, Permit2, EURC and Uniswap v4 are all at their canonical addresses. The test keys are funded.
   - **T-REX.** T-REX Network 5.0.0-beta2 with ONCHAINID 2.2, and each agent limited by `setAgentRestrictions` (ADR 0085).
   - **The deposit.** An allowlist ERC-20 like JPMD, eligible by the bank's own identity registry (ADR 0086).
   - **Licences.** A narrow GPL-3.0 boundary, and none of Tokeny's non-commercial modules (ADR 0087).
1. **ERC-3643 core.** *Contracts done (ADR 0088); the root scripts and the site follow in later workstreams.*
   - T-REX as a dependency, with ONCHAINID identities created by an identity factory.
   - The Claim Topics and Trusted Issuers registries.
   - The Identity Registry and its storage.
   - Modular compliance.
   - The structured note becomes a T-REX token.
   - Our Identity Registry, InstrumentGate and grant flags are retired.
2. **Identity and onboarding by role, without the credential platform.** *Done (ADR 0089); the pool's gate was done in workstream 6, brought forward (ADR 0090).*
   - The five portals, and each custody model.
   - The client's consent to share its bank KYB claim.
   - Claim issuers for the bank and the onboarding agent, with the verified records kept off-chain in each portal (Q1).
   - Expiry in the claim issuer (Q3).
   - Wallet control by typed signature (Q4).
   - The routes rebuilt on claims: bank custody, a third-party custodian, self-custody, and an existing identity brought to the note (Q2).
   - The platform's modules, scripts, settings and names removed (Q6).
3. **The factory.** One act issues a note's token and its lifecycle contracts on the shared
   scaffolding, replacing the site's nineteen issuance transactions. *Done (ADR 0091): the Issuer's
   one transaction, then the bank's two and the Agent's one.*
4. **The lifecycle on ERC-3643.**
   - Record dates and the stop on transfers become a compliance module.
   - The Paying Agent and the locked settlement act as agents. T-REX's agent role is broad (mint, burn, forced transfer, freezing, recovery), so each is limited to what it needs.
   - The governance controller holds ownership.
   - S-25's lost-key correction uses ERC-3643's wallet recovery.
   - Determinations are unchanged.

   *Done: built with the core (ADRs 0085, 0088), owned through the note factory until the ceremony
   hands it to the controller (ADR 0091). Workstream 4 added the proof the browser lacked: the
   governance spec now recovers a lost wallet and freezes and unfreezes an account from the Tier 1
   page. That found a defect, now fixed: a governance wallet's second act in quick succession
   could sign over a nonce already used, read from a node a block behind, and be refused (GS026);
   each act now waits until the wallet's new nonce reads back.*
5. **The tokenized deposit,** rebuilt to the design workstream 0 settles: credited and debited by
   the bank, allowlisted, with bank-managed identities for the bank's clients. *Done: the token with
   the core (ADRs 0086, 0088); the bank's acts on it, debit, hold, pause and a move by order with
   its reason, on a Deposit page in its portal (ADR 0092).*
6. **The pool, on by default** (amended, ADR 0090): EURC on Ethereum Sepolia, its gate reading the
   same identities (Q5). *Gate, scripts, governance, portals and tests done (ADR 0090); EURC on Ethereum Sepolia follows in workstream 10.*
7. **Day-to-day reconciliation.** The close-out statement (ADR 0084) generalised into a
   reconciliation statement at any block: positions, cash, open offers, distributions due and
   held. It can be downloaded daily, and says closed once the note has redeemed. *Done (ADR 0094):
   every note the factory issued, as at the close of any day, now or a block, in JSON and CSV.*
8. **The term sheet as data.**
   - A term-sheet record with every field typed and any field allowed to be "not applicable", its hash anchored on-chain as today.
   - Built from our instrument terms now, then fitted to the bank's field list when it arrives.
9. **The order of lifecycle events.** A precise sequence in the overview, fitted to the bank's
   detailed order when it arrives.
10. **Deployment.**
    - A fresh Ethereum Sepolia deployment.
    - A container image of the site for the bank's virtual machine, with mocks for anything it cannot reach.
    - An IT hand-over guide.
    - An AI build brief: the requirements and design written for the bank's AI-led process, pointing at this repository as the reference.
11. **Documents.**
    - The overview rewritten for 0.2.0: ERC-3643, the roles and portals, the deposit, the factory, the nine success criteria, Ethereum Sepolia, and the conversion pool on the same identities (ADR 0090).
    - A new PDF.
    - The ADRs for every decision above.
12. **Release 0.2.0.** All tests on anvil, the deployment, a tagged release, and the bank's IT
    team given access to the repository.

## Risks

- **Licence.** T-REX's GPL-3.0 beside our MPL-2.0. The boundary is kept narrow and stated plainly for
  the bank's legal review (ADR 0087).
- **A pre-release T-REX.** 5.0.0-beta2 gives scoped agents, but it is a beta whose audit is in
  progress. It is pinned exactly, and every restriction is tested (ADR 0085). Its implementation
  authority lets anyone switch versions; we deploy our own fixed authority in its place (ADR 0088),
  and the defect should be reported to the T-REX Network privately.
- **Unaudited community code** for the deposit's freezing (ERC-7943). It is kept small, vendored at
  a pinned commit, and tested fully (ADR 0086).
- **Ethereum Sepolia's fees and faucets** are less generous than Base Sepolia's. Every key needs
  test ETH, and the Keys page's thresholds need retuning.
- **Time.** The bank's IT team aims at a first cut in late October or early November, so 0.2.0
  should land before they start building.
