# Release 0.3.0: a note programme run by an agency and trust business

## Why

The audience is a global bank's agency and trust business: issuing and paying agent, registrar
and trustee for a note programme. It issues many notes, sometimes several at once, and manages each
through its life under one governance framework, every act naming the issue it concerns.

A review on 16 October 2026 found that 0.2.0 was designed around one demonstration note. Three
examples:
- **Governance.** It is tied to the first note, and that note's controller also holds the deposit's and the pool's administration, with no act to use it.
- **The Issuer's key.** One hot key can change any note the factory still holds.
- **Roles.** Each role is held note by note, so a rotation takes one act per note.

The site, likewise, runs one note at a time, by one person, with every page re-reading the whole
book. The owner chose to address all of it.

## Order

1. **Wording.** *Done:* what users see explains itself in plain English, without citing decision records, and stale text is gone.
2. **Programme contracts** (workstreams 1–7). One fresh Ethereum Sepolia deployment at the end, verified.
3. **The desk** (workstreams 8–13), partly alongside step 2.
4. **Decision records** (workstream 14), last, so they describe the final design.
5. **Release** (workstream 15).

## Workstreams

Status as built at release 0.3.0. What is not built is carried to the next release.

### Programme contracts

1. **One programme controller.** *Done.*
   - Acts name the note, checked against the notes it governs.
   - The factory hands every note over at issuance, in the same transaction.
   - Typed acts administer the deposit's and the gateway's roles.
   - The typed acts and written reasons, the two tiers, the expiry and the institution recovery stay as they are.
2. **The Issuer only issues.** *Done.*
   - The factory is owned by the Issuer's multi-signature wallet.
   - Once a note is issued, nothing about it is administered by the Issuer.
   - The Paying Agent's administration is the controller's.
3. **A programme register of roles,** consulted by every note's contracts, so a rotation is one act.
   *Partly done:* the servicing wallet, the Calculation Agent and the funder are in the register,
   each rotated for every note by one Tier 2 act. *Not built:* the payer and the onboarding agent.
4. **Cheap, isolated, batched issuance.** *Done.* Each note keeps its own lifecycle contracts, deployed as minimal clones of fixed implementations, and several notes can be issued in one call. Issuing one note costs about 5.7 million gas, down from about 10.5 million.
5. **Supervision across the book.** *Done.* A freeze on one note or many, and wallet recovery across every note the wallet holds, each one Tier 1 act; a programme-wide sanctions module bound to every note.
6. **The shared identity storage, governed.** *Done.*
   - The controller, by Tier 2 act, retires a redeemed note's registry from the storage and can hand the storage to a successor factory.
   - Notes' registries read the storage but cannot write to it.
7. **Per-note money and identity.** *Done:*
   - each distribution funded by an exact approval when it opens;
   - a Tier 2 act to correct a determination, reversing and restating a paid coupon. It is refused while the date's coupon is being paid, and, in the contract, once the date's redemption has opened; a test shows the first refusal, and none yet the second;
   - a series number on every note and an optional ISIN, in the factory's record and on its identity;
   - optional extra claims per series, up to four, on top of the common note claim.

### The desk

8. **Submission and jobs.** *Done.* One sender per key across servers, under a lease in the database. Issuance and payment runs are background jobs, carried on after the response, by staff page loads and by a daily cron on Vercel's Hobby plan, protected by `CRON_SECRET`.
9. **The book view.** *Partly done:* the Book shows every note on one page, with units outstanding and in inventory, dates determined, what each needs next (a determination or a payment, marked when due), and proposals awaiting approval, jobs running and jobs failed. *Not built:*
   - funding against what is due;
   - positions per client on the Book (holdings across every note are on the participants page);
   - offers expiring and amounts held;
   - an audit log filtered by note and by person (the log shows the latest 200 entries).
10. **Issue from a term sheet** staff upload, with its ISIN, checked before anything is sent; or a demonstration note from a scenario, with an offer period before the first date. Levels for determinations are entered with their source. *Done.*
11. **An index of notes and events** in the database, updated as blocks arrive, three blocks held back on a public network, so no page re-reads the book from the chain. *Done.*
12. **Roles per function** (Calculation Agent, payments), with a second person approving determinations and payments, or an automatic second approver by setting (`FOUR_EYES=auto`, the default, or `strict`). *Done.*
13. **Every view across every note:** the register reconciliation, participants and holdings, and closing checks. *Done.*

### Records and release

14. **Decision records consolidated.** *Done:* 27 current records replace the 119, describing the design as it stands, with no history of removed designs. They are for the codebase only, and nothing a user sees cites them.
15. **Release 0.3.0.** *Done:*
    - the full local suite;
    - a fresh Ethereum Sepolia deployment on 17 October 2026, verified on Etherscan, clones and linked libraries included;
    - the site pointed at it;
    - the overview, the lifecycle sequence, the IT hand-over guide and the AI build brief updated;
    - a signed tag, `v0.3.0`.

## Decisions for the owner

Answered 16 October 2026, and recorded in the decision records:

| # | Decision | Chosen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How a note's lifecycle contracts are deployed | **Minimal clones per note.** Each note keeps its own determinations, record dates, Paying Agent and settlement, isolated from every other note |
| 2 | Who owns the factory, and so who may issue | **The Issuer's multi-signature wallet.** Two of its three signers issue. Once issued, a note belongs to the programme controller |
| 3 | How each distribution is funded | **An exact approval when it opens.** The Agent's account stays pooled, but a note's Paying Agent may draw only the amount due, approved when the distribution opens |
| 4 | Whether a determination may be corrected | **At any time, through Tier 2.** The Calculation Agent re-signs the Levels. Once paid, the act also reverses and restates the payments, holder by holder |
| 5 | A note's identifier | **Both.** The factory assigns a unique programme series ID, and staff may enter an ISIN; both go in the record and on the note's identity |
| 6 | Which acts need a second person | **Determinations and payments.** Issuance and Tier 1 acts rest on their wallets' own signatures |
